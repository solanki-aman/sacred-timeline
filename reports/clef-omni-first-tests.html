What was tested

Clef-Omni is a decision model. It takes text, images, audio or video plus a set of closed questions, and returns a probability for each possible answer. It does not write text. In sacred-timeline it is the candidate for judging footage locally, so film files never leave the Mac.

  • Model: mlx-community/clef-omni-8bit, 33 GB on disk.
  • Software: Python 3.12, mlx 0.32.4, mlx-vlm 0.7.12, transformers 5.23.0.
  • Method: each clip was sent with all its questions in one request. Timings exclude the 2-second model load and one warm-up request.

What this shows

  • It sees real footage accurately. Clothing, setting, shot type, on-screen text and a small corner logo were all read correctly, each with probability above 0.9.
  • It hears dialogue. With no picture, it still picked the right paraphrase of the line and the right final word.
  • It is fast enough for indexing. About 1 second for a 6.8-second clip suggests roughly 17 minutes for a two-hour film at 16 questions per segment. That is an extrapolation from one clip, not a measurement.

What this does not show

  • The sample is tiny. Two clips and 31 questions. The real clip is a clean, well-lit close-up with one speaker, which is about as easy as real footage gets.
  • The questions were easy. Wrong options were clearly wrong. Telling two similar scenes apart, or recognising a specific character, is untested.
  • 3 answers have no reference. The checker could see frames but not hear the sound, so background music and the number of speakers were not verified. On music the two runs also disagree in strength: 0.06 with the picture and 0.22 with audio only. Both lean "no", but the gap is worth a closer look.
  • No hard cases. Dark scenes, fast cuts, overlapping speech, loud music under dialogue and clips longer than a few seconds were not tried.

Next tests worth running

  • A full scene of several minutes, split into segments, to measure real indexing speed.
  • Questions with near-miss options, to see where the probabilities stop being trustworthy.
  • A transcript from a speech-to-text model as an independent reference for the audio answers.
